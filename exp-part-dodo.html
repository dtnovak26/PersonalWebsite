Deep Dive: What I Did

Introduction

Dodo was the first project I got to work on when I joined the Purdue Aerial Robotics Team (PART). After my first semester at Purdue I was unhappy with the technical team I had joined. So second semester I found where PART met, promptly showed up, knocked on the door, and asked to help out. James Wadhwani warmly welcomed me in and I have been hooked ever since. I joined after the CDR of Dodo, the R&D teams 2024/25 project and at the start of the manufacturing process. This hands-on approach helped me learn how to make better design decisions, and just in general how to build an aircraft.

What I Contributed

Dodo is a tiltrotor UAV, meaning the motors can rotate to transition between vertical takeoff/landing and efficient forward flight. this also means it is much more complex than a simple bulsa RC craft. Dodo is primarily a composite built aircraft flying with the test of full foam wings. I was heavily invovled in the manufacturing of these foam wings in which we determined it was much more of a hassle than doing a composite wingskin. This is where I got my first experience in composites and learned how to do wet layups, vacuum bagging, and the general process of making composite parts. In the spring all of the subteams come together to make a final manufacturing push. This was really great to get to learn from a bunch of different engineering disciplines how their focus areas impacted the aircraft. I also got to work hands on with a lot of the internal fuselage structure manufacturing as well as tail section. I made it my goal to learn something from every meeting, and contribute wherever the leads needed the most help. This mindset proved to be incredibly helpful.

Our first hover flight test was a success! The tricopter was successful in vertical flight and was pretty stable making patterned movements around. Our first horizontal flight test was a failure. We failed to prep our ardupilot gains and controls adequately before testing a horizontal takeoff at an undersized runway. This was a tough lesson to learn as it shattered our landing gear but we built back within a week. I was also able to contribute here to the avionics troubleshooting that was neccessary for us to confidently get back in the air due to some of my FTC experience.

Biking the 25 minutes up to the PTC, staying until 2 AM, biking back, and promptly showing up to my 7:30's the next day was honestly my favorite part of my freshman year at Purdue. I was able to learn so much and really excel in the rapid prototyping environment that PART provides. Throughout this work, we were able to get successful test flights and it was incredibly satisfying to watch and be a part of. Around this time I was asked to become the next testing team lead which I gladly accepted.

One of my first challenges in learning this new role was recovering from another fatal crash where we snapped both tiltrotors, rear boom, landing gear, and left wing. This also happened right as we neared the end of the semester with finals. Being able to be a pivotal part of this recovery was a huge learning experience for me, and it made it that much more rewarding watching our final successful test flight of the year where we deployed a successful payload (even if I should have been studying for finals at the time. )

Why It Matters

Dodo taught me to never stop learning, challenge every engineering decision, and work hard. PART defined a work-hard family like culture that sets it apart from other organizations, and I am truly glad that I joined to be a part of the organization.
