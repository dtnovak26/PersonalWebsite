Deep Dive: What I Did

Introduction

Kiwi was designed to build off of PART R&D's 2025 aircraft Dodo and be a viable back up for the SUAS/C-UASC competition. As the testing team lead I had the challenge of starting a team from scratch with 0 returning members, and defining tangible projects that would help the other subteams effectively. Through steep goals and effective outreach, we were able to build a team of ~10 members and successfully complete both wind tunnel and composite structures testing.

Wind Tunnel Testing

PART had never done wind tunnel testing before so we had to start from the basics. This was really cool to me as a MechE where I dont learn as complex of fluid dynamics in class as Aero students do. We started by learning how wind tunnels work, and what data we wanted to get to effectively influence the design of the aircraft. From that, we build a simple technology demonstrator aircraft that was to be tested to see if we could get usable data from the wind tunnel: the answer was yes. We were able to utilize the Boeing subsonic wind tunnel on campus to get effective CL, Cd, and CM graphs that roughly matched our expected results from CFD & airfoil calcs (XFLR5).

After we felt comfortable with this, we moved into replicating the actively changing design of Kiwi to put through the wind tunnel also in the fall semester. This was difficult as we had a constant loop of feedback from our testing, design requirements, and CFD that made one model hard to make. Despite this, we implemented some major improvements ilke tighter tolerance mounting, a more intuitive subtractive mounting technique, better control surface swap time (5 minutes down to 30 seconds), and more advaned manufacturing techniques. These were all implemented in our final wind tunnel testing towards the end of the semester where we were able to present out CD, CM, and CL findings at CDR. There were still some gaps in our findings which we met with professors and an industry professional from Lockheed Martins Skunk Works to diagnose. Overall, I am really proud of how this project turned out, and how we effectively proved that PART can and should use wind tunnel testing in years to come.

Composite Structures Testing

One of PART's unique draws as a UAV club has been our advanced composites manufacturing techniques. With renewed support this year we took on advancing these techniques to the next level and structurally using carbon fiber, instead of just as stron shell supports. The testing team took to do this my doing tensile characterization testing in the form of coupons, as well as more advanced core implementation, molds, and more. I led these projects in companionship with graduate student Tony Wu whos masters is focussed on composites. This was a great learning expereince for the team, and for me as a leader being able to elevate his expertise to the rest of the club who had never even done layups before. One of my favorite parts of this project was teaching composite layups to the new members and getting feedback in real time! By the end of the semester, the main projects we had done (composite coupons, advanced molds, ply-mapped structures) had been proven enough to be used on Kiwi for spring manufacturing, successfully proving the testing teams goal for the fall.

Looking Forward

I was extremely grateful to be trusted with the R&D testing team lead position, and am proud as to how far I was able to grow the team in a single semester. That being said, I tooka co-op offer at Firefly Aerospae to be a composites ME and handed the reigns off. That being said I did not want my experience with PART nor with leadership to be done at this point. I stayed actively involved with the team during my internship and was able to help remotely with the manufacturing of Kiwi. My favorite part of this semester on PART was at the very end when we had a club-wide meeting on how to improve the organization. I took lead of that meeting and led it from the front of the room making action items and challenging for better feedback. It was in that moment where I decided to run for president of the organization for the next academic year; which I can now proudly say I am the incoming president after elections have concluded! I am incredibly excited to take on this role and grow the club even further from what it is now!
